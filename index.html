Katharina Mayer

Data Science · NLP and LLMs · Law and Economics

I work on natural language processing and large language models, often with legal text. My background combines economics, law and data science, so I can work both with the methods and with the domain behind the data.

After my master's in Social and Economic Data Science at the University of Konstanz, I now work there as a research assistant on detecting AI-generated legal text, which includes generating synthetic texts with open-source LLMs and building a benchmark dataset for detection methods.

CourtListener Research Database

2026

A reduced relational database of US judges, judicial careers and court opinions, derived from the CourtListener bulk data. It adds the attribution of opinions to their authoring judges and links appellate cases to Federal Judicial Center records.

CodeData on Zenodo

Gender bias in judicial language

2026 · Master's thesis

Examines gender associations in US federal appellate opinions and whether large language models reproduce them. The analysis combines word embeddings trained on 1.14 million opinions with a counterfactual experiment in which four LLMs decide identical cases that differ only in the gender of a party.

AI-generated comments on YouTube

2025

Examines whether AI-generated comments differ in sentiment from human-written ones in YouTube discussions on climate change. Around 68,000 comments from four channels were classified with a DeBERTa-based AI detector and a fine-tuned RoBERTa sentiment model, and analysed with multinomial logistic regression.
